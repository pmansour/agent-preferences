# Search workflow

## Prepare the search map

Record permitted departure/return windows, minimum/maximum nights, must-be-there dates, number and types of passengers, cabins, maximum stops/duration, and prohibited airports/connections. Count nights from local destination arrival/departure, not simply origin departure dates. Resolve relative dates in the user's timezone and show the explicit year.

Group airport choices into same-city alternatives, reasonable ground-access alternatives, and flights requiring positioning. Do not silently equate distant airports. Check transport time, material border/visa requirements, baggage recheck, and airport changes. Do not guess nationality or ask for passport details without a real need.

Map plausible operating carriers/hubs using observed schedules. Build:

`transferable bank points / existing miles → booking program → operating airline → feasible route`

Verify each arrow separately. Alliance membership is a discovery clue, not proof of inventory, transferability, cabin access, or stopover rights. Include bilateral partners. Inaccessible programs may supply leads but not funded recommendations.

## Pass A: cash and calendar discovery

- Start with Google Flights or another broad fare search, then airline websites. Check passenger mix, baggage/seating needs, fare family, and premium economy as a distinct cabin when relevant.
- Use departure/return grids across the requested range and allowed trip lengths. For multi-city trips, search the complete ordered itinerary plus useful open-jaw constructions.
- Record the cheapest practical cash choice and shortest/most convenient reasonable choice; inspect a premium alternative when it could materially improve the journey.
- Independently scan award calendars at plausible programs for each direction or long-haul bottleneck. Check operating-carrier and reachable partner programs where useful.
- Do not restrict award searches to cheap cash dates. Do not choose a return solely on price if it violates trip length.
- Prefer month views covering all requested days, followed by drill-down. Without calendars, use bounded daily batches. For huge ranges, use a disclosed rotating-weekday sample to find promising areas, then deepen them; a sample does not establish the best date in the entire window.
- Keep a small task-local notebook of ranges/programs searched, outcomes, and booking-window limits. Unsearched or blocked areas must not become “no availability.”

## Pass B: construct complete trips

Test constructions justified by the route, not every permutation:

| Construction | Purpose | Verify |
|---|---|---|
| Round-trip cash | International returns can undercut two one-ways | Exact outbound + inbound together, never half a return fare |
| Two one-ways | Mix airlines/payment methods | Both directions and all restrictions/costs |
| Open jaw / multi-city | Different arrival/departure cities or required visits | Complete fare versus separate parts plus ground travel |
| Cash out + award back, and reverse | Good award space may exist only one way | Complete hybrid versus best complete cash alternative |
| Asymmetric cabins | Spend where sleep/comfort matters most | Long-haul and connecting cabins each direction |
| Stopover award | A required second city may fit routing rules | Current rule, full quote, extra points/cash and stop costs |
| Positioning + award | An alternate gateway can unlock seats | Feeder, buffer, hotel, bags, airport changes and missed-connection exposure |
| Multi-carrier / RTW | Genuine multi-region trips | Current product, routing, funding and taxes; cash products differ from mileage awards |

Search complete origin–destination connections first. If unsuccessful, search long-haul and feeders to locate the bottleneck, then reassemble and **reprice the whole itinerary**. Married-segment logic means separately visible seats may not combine, and connecting awards may exist when individual segments do not.

Never silently insert hidden-city/skiplagging, speculative upgrades, waitlists, or separate-ticket connections. Show an upgrade option only when fare eligibility, cost and confirmable upgrade inventory are established. “Business” or “first” does not guarantee lie-flat seating.

Keep the party together. If seats are insufficient, first try nearby dates, cabins, routings and programs. Splitting reservations/cabins/flights requires disclosure and the user's willingness; keep children with appropriate adults. Two seats plus two seats is not proof four can be booked without repricing or a race for shared inventory.

## Pass C: portals and funding

Check strong flight/date candidates in the user's relevant card portals. Capture portal **cash**, **points**, cash+points slider quotes, fare family and offer eligibility. Paying bank points through a portal buys a revenue ticket; it is distinct from transferring for an airline award. Verify earning/servicing implications for the actual fare.

Compare viable transfer paths from the user's actual points programs, existing miles, current bonuses, increments and timing. Allocate balances across the **whole trip**: the same points balance cannot fund two legs simultaneously. Alternative proposals may each assume the full balance but are mutually exclusive.

Do not presume pooling between people or transfers between airline programs. Verify account ownership, household/authorized-user rules, name matching and account-age restrictions only for the paths in use.

## Pass D: verify finalists

Reach the relevant airline/program or seller's detailed price summary for the actual party without making a transaction. Verify:

- All segments with flight numbers, operating carriers, local dates/times/day offsets, each layover and total elapsed time.
- Cabin per segment, fare family, baggage and important seat restrictions.
- Enough seats at the **quoted party total**. Never multiply a single-seat promotional rate to claim a group quote.
- Issuing seller/program, original currencies, all points/cash due, mandatory extras, and material refund/change/redeposit terms.
- Funding reachability and balances. If unknown, label funding conditional even when space is visible.
- Same-flight/date/passenger/cabin cash comparison with matched practical inclusions. If terms differ materially, call it approximate instead of passing a definitive CPP test.

For codeshares, match physical operating flights rather than demanding identical marketing numbers. Distinguish itinerary-level quotes from segment inventory, and live quotes from issued tickets.

Use precise statuses:

- **Verified quote:** complete itinerary and party price visible at the relevant source, timestamped; still subject to change.
- **Lead:** calendar/cache/chart/partial trip or unverified passenger count.
- **Unavailable in checked search:** correct live search completed with no matching seats for that scope.
- **Not yet released:** outside the booking window, not sold out.
- **Blocked / unknown / unsearched:** failed UI, login barrier, missing taxes/balance, incomplete coverage, or untouched dates.

## Browser efficiency and resilience

Use one tab per logical search when supported; record filters and inspect fresh state before acting. Parallelize only independent surfaces when allowed, never racing one tab or shared account workflow. Keep request volumes modest.

On failure, inspect visible state, retry a plausible correction, then try another source or request the human login step. Do not install stealth browsers, harvest cookies, disable protections, solve challenges through undocumented workarounds, or modify the user's browser setup. Use the current tool's supported human handoff.

Optional aggregators (Seats.aero, Roame, point.me, PointsYeah, Award Travel Finder, Gondola) can accelerate discovery when available. Preserve timestamps, monitored-route limits, aggregation semantics and pagination. Never combine a summary's minimum miles with maximum seats belonging to a different flight.

Stop expanding when there are useful distinct verified proposals and relevant coverage is recorded. If the range is too broad to finish, return verified results with specific gaps and a focused continuation. Do not claim a global optimum or silently spend hours on unrelated permutations.
